From web to app: architecture & bottom bar

What this is. This map shows the navigation for the new Rasmus mobile app: a bottom bar with 5 main destinations that covers everything a user does today, always within easy reach. The goal is to bring two products into a single app: the auctions from Rasmus Auctions and the live auctions from BidCloud, which join the bottom bar as one more tab called Rasmus TV. Instead of copying the website's hamburger menu, we started from what people actually do and turned it into clear places to go. The result is a simpler app: five clear places, with everything the user does living inside the one it belongs to.

03

Good ideas to discuss — things we could integrate

Beyond the core screens, here are a few proven patterns from Whatnot and eBay that could make the app richer. They're not decided — just worth discussing.

Idea 1 · Discovery

A richer search experience

Right now search is fairly bare. When the user taps the search bar, instead of showing an empty screen we could greet them with browsable categories first, and then, once they focus the field, surface suggestions and recently viewed auctions. This nudges people to explore and stumble onto items they'll like, rather than only finding what they already know to type.

Idea 2 · Convenience

Saved searches

Both Whatnot and eBay let users save a search and come back to it later. For Rasmus this fits naturally: someone hunting for a specific kind of lot could save that search and re-run it in one tap, instead of re-typing filters every visit.

Idea 3 · Activity

Save whole auctions, not just items

Today watching is item-by-item. Whatnot lets users save an entire show, not only individual products. We could do the same: let people follow a full auction from Activity, so they get reminded when it's closing — useful for both standard auctions and Rasmus TV lives.
